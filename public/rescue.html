Why choose adoption?

Why adopt a rescue animal?

At PawPal, we believe that every animal deserves a family. Whether you're looking for a playful puppy, a gentle senior dog, a curious cat or a unique companion, choosing to adopt can make a lasting difference in both your life and theirs.

Thousands of animals enter Australian shelters and rescue groups every year. Many are healthy, loving pets who simply ran out of luck, and others have overcome neglect or cruelty with the help of vets, carers and volunteers. All they need now is someone to take them home.

Adopt, don't shop. Give love, save lives.
  • Save a life and give a second chanceYour adoption gives an animal the safe, happy life every pet deserves.
  • Reduce overcrowdingEvery adoption frees a space in a shelter or rescue centre for another animal in need.
  • A strong, grateful bondRescue pets often form a deep connection with the people who give them a home.
  • Support ethical pet ownershipAdopting stands against puppy farms and backyard breeding and supports animal welfare.
  • The joy of seeing them thriveWatching a rescued animal learn to trust, play and relax is one of life's great rewards.
  • Great valueAdoption fees usually include vaccinations, desexing, microchipping and a vet check, which would cost far more on their own.
Where they come from

What "rescue" really means

Rescue animals reach PawPal's partner shelters in many ways. Their past doesn't define them, but knowing it helps us find the right home.

Neglect and cruelty cases

Animals removed by inspectors after being underfed, chained up or left without care. With vet treatment and kindness, most recover fully.

Strays and lost animals

Pets found wandering and never claimed by an owner. Many are already house-trained and simply waiting to be someone's pet again.

Retired racing and working animals

Greyhounds retired from racing and working dogs that need a slower life. Greyhounds in particular make famously calm, gentle companions.

Hoarding situations

Animals rescued from homes with far too many pets to care for. They are often shy at first, but blossom with patience.

Farm and battery rescues

Ex-battery hens, goats and other farm animals given the chance to live out their lives on grass, in the sun.

Owner surrenders

Pets given up because of a move, illness, a new baby or a change in work. They have usually lived in a family home and settle quickly.

Rehabilitation

From rescue to forever home

Every rescue animal on PawPal has been through the same careful journey before they are listed for adoption.

  1. RescueThe animal is taken into safe care by inspectors, rangers, a shelter or a rescue group.
  2. Vet careA full health check, treatment, vaccinations, desexing, microchipping and parasite control.
  3. RehabilitationTime with experienced carers or a foster family to rebuild trust, confidence and good habits.
  4. Ready for adoptionThe shelter writes an honest profile: personality, needs and the kind of home that would suit them best.
  5. Forever homeYou apply, meet them, and take them home with a first-30-days care plan and ongoing shelter support.

Bringing them home

Give them time to settle

Rescue animals need a little patience while they learn that they are safe. Many carers use the "3-3-3" guide as a rough idea of what to expect.

  • The first 3 days: they may be nervous, hide, or eat less. Keep things quiet and let them explore at their own pace.
  • The first 3 weeks: they start to learn your routine and show more of their personality. Keep meals, walks and bedtime predictable.
  • The first 3 months: most feel truly at home, trusting you and settling into family life.

Every animal is different, and these timings are only a guide. Your shelter is always happy to help.

Is a rescue animal right for you?

  • You can offer a calm, predictable routine for the first few weeks.
  • You're happy to be patient if they are shy or unsure at first.
  • You'll follow the shelter's advice about their history and needs.
  • You're ready to celebrate small wins, like the first tail wag or purr.

Not sure? Describe your home and routine and PawPal will suggest animals that fit.

Myths and facts

What people often get wrong about rescue pets

"Rescue animals have behaviour problems."

Most animals end up in rescue because of human circumstances, such as a move, a new baby or a lost pet, not because of anything they did. Shelters assess every animal and describe their personality honestly.

"You can't find puppies or purebreds in rescue."

Shelters care for animals of every age, and many purebreds and rare breeds come through rescue too. Browse our Rare & Special Breeds to see for yourself.

"You never know what you're getting."

Rescue animals are vet checked and spend time with carers before adoption, so shelters often know more about their personality than a breeder could tell you about a young puppy.

"Adopting is complicated."

On PawPal you can apply online in about ten minutes, follow every step on a timeline, book your meet and greet and get a care plan when you're approved.
