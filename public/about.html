How it started

PawPal began with a dog who came back twice

In 2024 a shelter volunteer watched the same young kelpie return twice in five months. Both families had loved him. Neither had understood that a working dog in a second-floor apartment, alone for ten hours a day, was never going to settle. Nothing on his listing had told them: it showed a photograph, a breed and an age.

That is a common story, and it is rarely anybody's fault. Shelters are stretched, listings get written in a hurry, and adopters make one of the biggest decisions of an animal's life from three lines of text. The information that would have prevented both returns already existed — the care team knew he needed hours of exercise and hated being alone. It simply never reached the people reading the ad.

PawPal was built to close that gap. Every observation the care team records becomes a structured field: energy level, exercise needs, how long the animal copes alone, whether they have lived with children, dogs or cats. Those same fields drive the search filters, the profile page and the AI matching, so what staff know is what adopters see.

The kelpie was adopted a third time, by a courier who drives with him all day. He has been home two years.

Who we are

A small shelter team, a group of volunteers, and four students

PawPal is two things at once: a demonstration shelter, and the software project that runs it.

The shelter team

Four roles cover an animal's whole journey: intake and assessment, daily care and enrichment, adoptions and visits, and veterinary coordination. Every profile on this site is written from their notes.

Our volunteers

Dog walkers, cat socialisers, foster carers, photographers and event helpers. They give the animals the one thing a staff roster cannot stretch to — unhurried, one-to-one time.

The project team

Four Victoria University students built PawPal for the NIT3003/NIT3004 IT Capstone: Uniska Sthapit, Pratikshya Bhujel, Pemba Sange Sherpa and Sushrit Phuyal.

How we help

Six ways PawPal changes an animal's outcome

Rehoming is only part of it. Prevention, education and keeping animals with the owners they already have matter just as much.

Rescue and rehabilitation

Animals arrive as strays, surrenders or from cruelty cases. They are health-checked, treated and given weeks of care before they are listed — sometimes months.

Honest, structured matching

Explained compatibility scores, and profiles that say what is unknown rather than guessing. Fewer mismatches means fewer animals coming back.

Foster care

Kittens, puppies, nervous adults and recovering animals do far better in a home than a kennel. Our foster network makes that possible.

Cruelty prevention

Reporting lines, inspections, community desexing and education. Most cases end in advice and support rather than prosecution.

Report cruelty

Keeping pets with owners

Pet food support, subsidised veterinary care and temporary fostering help people through hardship without surrendering the animal they love.

Care after adoption

A vet finder, guidance on the first year of care, and a team you can still ring six months later when something changes.

Privacy policy

We collect only what is needed to process an adoption: your name, contact details and the answers you give in the inquiry form. Your answers are visible to the shelter team only and are never sold or shared with third parties.

Passwords are stored as secure one-way hashes, and you stay signed in with a protected cookie that page scripts cannot read. Pet medical and rescue notes are visible to staff only. To update or delete your information, contact us using the form above.
