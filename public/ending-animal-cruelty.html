Ending animal cruelty starts with someone making a call

Most cruelty is not dramatic. It is a dog left in a yard with no shelter, a cat nobody has fed for a fortnight, twenty animals in a house that got away from someone. If something looks wrong, report it — you do not need proof, and you do not need to be certain.

Help numbers

Who to contact, and when

Use the line that matches the situation. If an animal is in immediate danger, ring rather than email — a written report can wait hours for someone to open it.

If an animal is in immediate danger

An animal locked in a hot car, actively being harmed, badly injured or unable to stand needs an urgent response. Call the urgent line above straight away. Do not break into a vehicle or property yourself — tell the operator exactly where you are and stay nearby if it is safe to do so.

    Educational information

    What counts as animal cruelty

    Cruelty is not only deliberate violence. In every Australian state, failing to provide for an animal's basic needs is an offence in its own right.

    General educational information for this prototype. Animal welfare law differs between states and territories — check your local legislation for the wording that applies to you.

    The process

    What happens after you report

    People often hesitate because they do not know what they are setting in motion. Usually it is far less dramatic than they fear.

      Most cases end in education, not prosecution. The common outcome is advice, a direction to get veterinary treatment, and a follow-up visit. Animals are only removed where there is no safe alternative.

      Prevention

      Warning signs, and how prevention actually works

      Cruelty investigations are the last line. Most suffering is prevented earlier, by desexing, by affordable veterinary care, and by owners knowing what an animal needs before they take one home.
